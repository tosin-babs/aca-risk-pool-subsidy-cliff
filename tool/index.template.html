What this is

Read this before relying on a number

This calculator accompanies a working paper on what the expiry of the enhanced premium tax credits did to the individual-market risk pool. It uses the benchmark premium — the second-lowest-cost silver plan, which is what the tax credit is legally calculated against — taken from the CMS Exchange Rate and Plan Attributes public use files for plan years 2025 and 2026.

It shows a state median. Premiums are set by rating area, and within a state they vary widely; the chart shows your state's range where it can. Your own plan may not be the benchmark plan, and most people do not buy the benchmark. Cost-sharing reductions, which also depend on income, are not modelled.

It covers the 31 states that use HealthCare.gov. State-based exchanges — California, New York, Colorado, Massachusetts and others — file their own rates and are not in this data. Several of them run their own subsidy programmes that cushion the change, so their absence matters.

It is a single-person calculation. A household's benchmark is built from each member's rate and its poverty threshold rises with household size, so a family's numbers differ.

For an authoritative estimate of what you personally will pay, use HealthCare.gov or your state marketplace. Nothing here is advice.
